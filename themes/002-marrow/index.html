We cook what the boats and the market give us that morning, over one fire, for twenty‑four people. The menu is written at four in the afternoon. If something is not on it, it is because it was not good enough today.

01 / 04

The kitchen

One fire, no shortcuts, and a very small larder.

Everything that reaches a plate here has been near flame. There is no induction hob in the building and no sous-vide bath, which means timing is the whole discipline — a turbot waits for nobody, and neither does a table of eight.

Ife cooks; Tomas runs the room. Between them they have worked kitchens in Lisbon, Hobart and a fishing village outside Bergen, and the food here borrows from all three without pretending to belong to any of them. Bread is baked each afternoon in the same oven the lamb comes out of, which is why it tastes faintly of lamb, which we consider a feature.

03 / 04

The room

Bare tables, good chairs, one loud night a month.

A converted chandlery with the original windows and a floor that creaks in two places we have decided to keep. No music until ten, no tasting-menu theatre, and nobody will fold your napkin while you are in the bathroom.

On the last Friday of the month we push the tables together and cook one thing for everybody. Those evenings are loud and they sell out in a morning.
